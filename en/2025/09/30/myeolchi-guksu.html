Anchovy Noodle Soup (멸치국수, Myeolchi Guksu) Recipe

Welcome back to Chelsea's K-Food Recipes! Today, we're making a comforting Korean classic, Anchovy Noodle Soup (멸치국수, Myeolchi Guksu)!

Also known as Janchi Guksu (잔치국수), this light and flavorful noodle soup features thin wheat noodles served in a delicious broth made from dried anchovies and kelp.

In Korea, this dish is often enjoyed as a simple homemade meal or served during special celebrations. With its warm, savory broth, tender noodles, and colorful vegetable toppings, Myeolchi Guksu is the perfect comfort food for any season.

Let's get cooking!

🍜 Ingredients

For the Anchovy Broth:

  • 10-12 dried anchovies (멸치, myeolchi), heads and guts removed
  • 1 piece dried kelp (다시마, dashima), about 10 x 10 cm
  • 6 cups water
  • 1/2 onion, sliced
  • 2 green onions
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 1 tbsp soup soy sauce (국간장, guk-ganjang)
  • Salt, to taste

For the Noodles:

  • 200g thin wheat noodles (소면, somyeon)
  • 1/2 zucchini, julienned
  • 1/2 carrot, julienned
  • 2 eggs
  • 1 tsp vegetable oil

For the Seasoning Sauce (Optional):

  • 2 tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 tbsp chopped green onion
  • 1 tsp minced garlic
  • 1 tsp sesame oil
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds
  • 1/2 tsp Korean red pepper flakes (고춧가루, gochugaru)

Optional Garnishes:

  • Roasted seaweed (김, gim), cut into thin strips
  • Chopped green onions
  • Toasted sesame seeds
  • Kimchi, served on the side

👩‍🍳 Instructions

1️⃣ Prepare the Anchovy Broth

Remove the heads and dark innards from the dried anchovies to prevent bitterness.

In a large pot, add 6 cups of water, dried anchovies, kelp, onion, green onions, and garlic.

Bring to a gentle boil over medium-high heat.

After about 5 minutes of simmering, remove the kelp to prevent the broth from becoming slimy.

Continue simmering the remaining ingredients for another 10-15 minutes.

Strain the broth through a fine-mesh strainer and discard the solids.

Season with soup soy sauce and salt to taste.

Keep the broth warm over low heat.

2️⃣ Prepare the Vegetables

Cut the zucchini and carrot into thin, matchstick-sized strips.

Heat a small amount of vegetable oil in a frying pan over medium heat.

Lightly sauté the zucchini for 2-3 minutes until tender but still slightly crisp.

Remove from the pan and sauté the carrots for another 2-3 minutes.

Set the vegetables aside.

3️⃣ Prepare the Egg Garnish

Beat the eggs in a small bowl.

Lightly oil a nonstick frying pan and heat over low heat.

Pour in a thin layer of beaten egg and cook gently until set.

Flip carefully if needed, avoiding browning.

Let the egg cool slightly, then roll it up and slice it into thin strips.

This traditional Korean egg garnish is called Jidan (지단).

4️⃣ Cook the Noodles

Bring a large pot of water to a rolling boil.

Add 200g of somyeon noodles and cook according to the package instructions, usually about 3-4 minutes.

When the water begins to foam and rise, add a small splash of cold water to help prevent boiling over.

Once the noodles are cooked, drain them immediately.

Rinse thoroughly under cold running water, gently rubbing the noodles to remove excess starch.

This helps create the smooth, pleasantly chewy texture that makes Korean noodle soups so delicious.

Drain well.

5️⃣ Make the Seasoning Sauce

In a small bowl, combine:

  • Soy sauce
  • Chopped green onion
  • Minced garlic
  • Sesame oil
  • Toasted sesame seeds
  • Korean red pepper flakes

Mix everything together until well combined.

Set aside to serve with the noodle soup.

6️⃣ Assemble and Serve

Divide the cooked noodles evenly between two large serving bowls.

Pour the hot anchovy broth over the noodles.

Arrange the sautéed zucchini, carrots, and egg strips neatly on top.

Garnish with roasted seaweed strips, chopped green onions, and sesame seeds.

Serve immediately with the optional seasoning sauce and a side of kimchi.

Enjoy your delicious homemade Myeolchi Guksu!

💡 Tips for Perfect Myeolchi Guksu

  • ✅ Use good-quality dried anchovies: Large dried anchovies made for broth provide a rich, savory flavor without making the soup too fishy.
  • ✅ Remove the kelp early: Simmering kelp for too long can make the broth thick and slightly bitter.
  • ✅ Rinse the noodles thoroughly: Washing away the extra starch keeps the noodles springy and prevents the broth from becoming cloudy.
  • ✅ Keep the broth light: Traditional anchovy noodle soup should have a clean, delicate flavor rather than an overly salty taste.
  • ✅ Customize your toppings: Add mushrooms, spinach, or thinly sliced fish cakes for extra flavor and texture.
  • ✅ Make the broth ahead: Prepare the anchovy broth in advance and refrigerate it for up to 3 days or freeze it for later use.

Anchovy Noodle Soup (멸치국수, Myeolchi Guksu) is more than just a simple bowl of noodles. It's a comforting Korean dish that brings warmth and happiness to the table.

In Korean culture, long noodles symbolize longevity, which is why noodle dishes like Janchi Guksu have traditionally been served at weddings, birthdays, and other celebrations.

Whether you're looking for a quick lunch, a light dinner, or a cozy meal on a chilly day, this flavorful noodle soup is always a wonderful choice.

The combination of savory anchovy broth, tender wheat noodles, and colorful toppings makes every bite satisfying and delicious.
