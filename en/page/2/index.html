  • Korean Apple Potato Salad (사과 사라다, Sagwa Sarada) Recipe

    Welcome back to Chelsea's K-Food Recipes! Today, we're making one of Korea's most unusual yet surprisingly delicious dishes, Korean Apple Potato Salad (사과 사라다, Sagwa Sarada)! 🍎🥔 Now, here's something that might confuse my Western friends. Apples, potatoes, and mayonnaise. Together. In a salad. Sounds a little strange, right? But trust me, it's absolutely delicious! In Korea, we have this old-fashioned dish called Sarada (사라다), a Korean-style pronunciation of the English word "salad" that became associated with creamy, mayonnaise-based salads. Unlike a typical Western green salad, Korean sarada combines sweet, crunchy fruits with soft potatoes, vegetables, and a generous amount of mayonnaise. It's creamy, sweet, slightly tangy, and incredibly satisfying. This nostalgic dish reminds many Koreans of childhood, family gatherings, holiday meals, and the side dishes our mothers and grandmothers used to prepare. And while potato salads and fruit salads certainly exist in Western countries, this particular Korean combination has its own special charm. Let's make some delicious Korean apple potato sarada!

  • Shrimp Cracker Ramen and Cheese Ramen (새우깡 라면과 치즈 라면, Saeukkang Ramyeon and Cheese Ramyeon) Recipe

    Welcome back to Chelsea's K-Food Recipes! Today, we're making two fun and nostalgic Korean instant noodle variations: Shrimp Cracker Ramen (새우깡 라면, Saeukkang Ramyeon) and Cheese Ramen (치즈 라면, Cheese Ramyeon)! 🍜🧀🦐 If you've ever tried Shin Ramyun (신라면), you probably know that it's one of Korea's most famous instant noodles. It's spicy, savory, and incredibly satisfying. But there's one little problem. It can be pretty spicy! These days, spicy Korean noodles are popular all over the world, but not everyone enjoys that level of heat. And apparently, adding shrimp crackers or a slice of cheese to Shin Ramyun was one of those creative ways people tried to make it taste a little less spicy. I find that so interesting! Instead of simply using less seasoning, people experimented with ingredients they already had at home. A handful of Saeukkang (새우깡), Korea's famous shrimp-flavored crackers, or a slice of processed cheese could turn an ordinary bowl of spicy ramyeon into something completely different. The cheese version makes the broth creamier and richer, while the shrimp cracker version adds a fun seafood flavor and a slightly thicker texture as the crackers soften. Of course, neither addition actually removes the chili from the soup. But cheese can make the heat feel gentler, and adding shrimp crackers changes the balance of flavors. And honestly, sometimes the most memorable Korean comfort foods come from little experiments like these. Today, I'll show you both versions using a package of Shin Ramyun. They're incredibly easy, inexpensive, and perfect for those days when you're craving something warm and comforting. Let's get cooking!

Today, I'm sharing this easy, old-fashioned Korean-style bread pizza that reminds me of those happy afternoons at Grandma's house. Let's get cooking!

  • Easy Korean Egg and Onion Soup (계란 양파국, Gyeran Yangpa Guk) Recipe

    Welcome back to Chelsea's K-Food Recipes! Today, we're making one of the easiest Korean soups ever, Korean Egg and Onion Soup (계란 양파국, Gyeran Yangpa Guk)! 🥚🧅 There's something you should know about Korean people. We really, really love having soup with our rice! Of course, not every Korean meal needs soup, but for many of us, a bowl of warm soup makes a meal feel complete. Rice, a few side dishes, and a comforting bowl of guk (국, Korean soup). That's a classic Korean home-cooked meal. But what happens when you open your refrigerator and there's practically nothing inside? No beef. No tofu. No mushrooms. Not even enough vegetables to make a proper soup. Just a couple of eggs and an onion. Well, that's more than enough! This is exactly when Gyeran Yangpa Guk comes to the rescue. With just a few basic ingredients and about 10 minutes, you can make a surprisingly delicious, light, and comforting soup. The onion adds a natural sweetness to the broth, while the eggs create soft, fluffy ribbons that make every spoonful satisfying. It's the kind of simple soup Korean families throw together when they need something warm on the table without spending much time cooking. Let's make the easiest Korean soup ever!
