Korean Stuffed Pepper and Perilla Leaf Jeon (고추전과 깻잎전, Gochu Jeon and Kkaennip Jeon) Recipe

Welcome back to Chelsea's K-Food Recipes! Today, we're celebrating Chuseok (추석, Korean Thanksgiving) with two of my absolute favorite Korean holiday dishes: Stuffed Pepper Jeon (고추전, Gochu Jeon) and Stuffed Perilla Leaf Jeon (깻잎전, Kkaennip Jeon)! 🌶️🍃

Happy Chuseok!

Chuseok, also called Hangawi (한가위), is one of Korea's most important traditional holidays. It's a time when families gather, share delicious food, and celebrate the autumn harvest.

And if you've ever experienced a Korean holiday meal, you probably know one thing.

There's always a lot of jeon!

Jeon (전) refers to Korean pan-fried dishes, usually made by coating ingredients with flour and beaten eggs before cooking them in a frying pan.

There are so many different kinds.

Zucchini jeon, fish jeon, mushroom jeon, meat jeon, and so many more.

During Korean holidays, families often prepare large platters filled with different varieties.

But if you ask me which ones I love the most?

Gochu jeon and kkaennip jeon. Every single time!

I love the combination of juicy, savory meat filling with the fresh flavor of peppers and the wonderful aroma of Korean perilla leaves.

They're both delicious, and honestly, I could eat an entire plate of just these two.

The best part is that they use the same meat filling and the same simple coating method.

And here's something important that applies to almost every kind of traditional Korean egg-coated jeon.

First, coat the ingredients lightly with flour. Shake off the excess flour really well. Then dip them into beaten eggs.

That little step makes a huge difference!

Too much flour creates a thick, gummy coating instead of the delicate golden egg layer we want.

Today, I'll show you how to make both of my favorite holiday jeon using one delicious meat filling.

Let's celebrate Chuseok together!

🍎 Ingredients

For the Meat Filling (Makes approximately 16-20 pieces total):

  • 300g ground beef or ground pork
  • 150g firm tofu
  • 1/4 onion, finely chopped
  • 1 green onion, finely chopped
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 tsp sesame oil
  • 1/2 tsp salt, or to taste
  • 1/4 tsp black pepper
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds (optional)

For Gochu Jeon (고추전):

  • 5-6 Korean mild green peppers (오이고추 or 아삭이고추), or other mild green peppers
  • A portion of the prepared meat filling

For Kkaennip Jeon (깻잎전):

  • 10-12 fresh Korean perilla leaves (깻잎, kkaennip)
  • The remaining meat filling

For the Flour and Egg Coating:

  • 1/2 cup all-purpose flour, or as needed
  • 3 large eggs
  • A pinch of salt
  • Neutral cooking oil, for pan-frying

For the Optional Dipping Sauce:

  • 2 tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 tbsp water
  • 1 tbsp rice vinegar
  • 1/2 tsp Korean red pepper flakes (고춧가루, gochugaru)
  • 1/2 tsp toasted sesame seeds

👩‍🍳 Instructions

1️⃣ Prepare the Meat Filling

Start by preparing the tofu.

Wrap the firm tofu in a clean kitchen towel or paper towels and gently press out as much water as possible.

This step is important because excess moisture can make the meat filling too soft and watery.

Crumble the drained tofu into a large mixing bowl.

Add the ground beef or pork, finely chopped onion, green onion, and minced garlic.

Season with soy sauce, sesame oil, salt, black pepper, and sesame seeds.

Mix everything thoroughly until the ingredients are evenly combined and the filling becomes slightly sticky.

You can use your hands or a spoon.

The tofu makes the filling softer and lighter, while the meat adds a delicious savory flavor.

Set the filling aside while preparing the peppers and perilla leaves.

2️⃣ Prepare the Peppers for Gochu Jeon

Wash the green peppers and pat them completely dry.

Cut each pepper in half lengthwise.

Carefully remove the seeds and white membranes using a small spoon.

Try to keep the pepper halves intact so they can hold the filling.

For this recipe, I recommend using mild Korean green peppers rather than very spicy chili peppers.

You want the peppers to add a fresh, slightly sweet flavor without overpowering the meat.

Lightly dust the inside of each pepper half with flour.

Shake off the excess flour!

This thin layer helps the meat filling stick to the pepper.

3️⃣ Stuff the Peppers

Take a small amount of the prepared meat filling and press it gently into each pepper half.

Fill the peppers evenly, smoothing the surface with a spoon.

Avoid piling the meat too high.

A relatively thin, even layer helps the filling cook through without burning the egg coating.

Repeat until all the peppers are filled.

Set aside on a plate.

4️⃣ Prepare the Perilla Leaves

Wash the perilla leaves carefully under cold running water.

Pat them completely dry with paper towels.

Perilla leaves have a wonderful, distinctive aroma that makes kkaennip jeon especially delicious.

Place a leaf on a clean cutting board with the underside facing up.

Lightly dust the inside surface with flour.

Once again, shake off any excess flour.

Place a small spoonful of meat filling on one half of the leaf.

Spread the filling into a thin, even layer, leaving a little space around the edges.

Fold the other half of the leaf over the filling, like a little envelope.

Press gently to help the leaf hold its shape.

Repeat with the remaining leaves.

Don't overstuff them because the meat needs to cook evenly inside.

5️⃣ Prepare the Flour and Egg Coating

Place the flour in a shallow dish.

Crack 3 eggs into a separate bowl.

Add a small pinch of salt and beat until smooth.

Now comes one of my most important tips for making Korean jeon.

The coating should always be flour first, then egg.

Lightly coat each stuffed pepper and folded perilla leaf with flour.

Gently tap or shake off the excess flour.

You want only a thin, even dusting.

Then dip each piece into the beaten egg mixture, making sure the outside is lightly coated.

Don't skip shaking off the extra flour before dipping into the eggs!

This is the same basic method I use for all kinds of traditional Korean egg-coated jeon.

The flour helps the egg adhere, but too much flour makes the coating thick and heavy.

A light coating gives you that beautiful, soft, golden finish.

6️⃣ Pan-Fry the Gochu Jeon

Heat a large nonstick frying pan over medium-low heat.

Add enough cooking oil to lightly coat the bottom of the pan.

Carefully place the egg-coated stuffed peppers into the pan with the meat side facing down.

Cook for approximately 3-4 minutes, until the egg coating becomes golden and the meat begins to cook through.

Gently flip the peppers and cook the other side for another 3-4 minutes.

Adjust the heat as needed.

You want the peppers to become tender while the meat cooks completely.

Avoid using high heat because the egg coating can brown too quickly before the filling is fully cooked.

If necessary, cover the pan briefly with a lid to help the filling cook through.

The meat filling should reach a safe internal temperature of 71°C (160°F) for ground beef or pork.

Transfer the finished gochu jeon to a plate lined with paper towels.

7️⃣ Pan-Fry the Kkaennip Jeon

Using the same frying pan, add a little more oil if needed.

Dip each stuffed perilla leaf into the beaten eggs after lightly coating it with flour and shaking off the excess.

Place the coated leaves gently into the pan.

Cook over medium-low heat for approximately 3-4 minutes per side, depending on the thickness of the filling.

Flip carefully so the leaves don't tear or open.

The outside should become lightly golden, while the meat inside cooks all the way through.

Again, keep the heat moderate.

The goal is to cook the filling completely without burning the delicate egg coating or perilla leaves.

Once cooked, transfer the kkaennip jeon to a plate.

8️⃣ Make the Dipping Sauce

In a small bowl, combine:

  • 2 tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 tbsp water
  • 1 tbsp rice vinegar
  • 1/2 tsp gochugaru
  • 1/2 tsp sesame seeds

Mix well.

This simple dipping sauce adds a little tanginess that balances the rich, savory jeon.

You can also enjoy both kinds of jeon without any sauce because the meat filling is already seasoned.

9️⃣ Serve for Chuseok

Arrange the gochu jeon and kkaennip jeon on a large serving platter.

Serve warm with freshly cooked rice, kimchi, and other Korean holiday dishes.

They also taste delicious at room temperature, which is one reason jeon is so popular during Korean family gatherings.

And if you're anything like me, you might find yourself eating a few pieces straight from the frying pan before they even make it to the table!

Enjoy these two delicious Korean holiday favorites.

💡 Tips for Perfect Gochu Jeon and Kkaennip Jeon

  • ✅ Flour first, egg second: This is the classic coating method for many traditional Korean jeon dishes.
  • ✅ Always shake off excess flour: This is one of the most important steps! Too much flour creates a thick, pasty coating and prevents the egg from coating the ingredients beautifully.
  • ✅ Use one filling for both: Preparing a single meat filling makes it easy to cook several varieties of jeon without extra work.
  • ✅ Remove moisture from the tofu: Dry tofu helps the filling hold together and prevents the jeon from becoming soggy.
  • ✅ Don't overstuff: Keep the meat filling thin enough to cook evenly, especially inside the folded perilla leaves.
  • ✅ Use mild peppers: Korean mild green peppers are ideal for gochu jeon because they provide a fresh flavor without too much heat.
  • ✅ Cook over medium-low heat: Jeon should become gently golden, not dark brown. Lower heat gives the meat enough time to cook through.
  • ✅ Make sure the meat is fully cooked: Check that the filling reaches 71°C (160°F) before serving.
  • ✅ Prepare ahead for the holidays: You can assemble the stuffed peppers and leaves in advance, refrigerate them, and coat them with flour and eggs just before frying.
  • ✅ Reheat in a frying pan: Leftover jeon tastes best when gently reheated in a lightly oiled pan until hot throughout.

Some people love zucchini jeon.

There's something about the combination of savory meat and fresh vegetables that I absolutely love.

Gochu jeon has that delicious contrast between the tender pepper and juicy meat filling.

Kkaennip jeon has the unmistakable aroma of Korean perilla leaves, which makes every bite feel extra special.

And both are wrapped in that familiar, delicate coating of flour and eggs.

It's such a simple technique, but it makes all the difference.

A little flour, shake it off really well, then dip it in beaten eggs.

That's the method I always come back to.
