Korean Steamed Eggs (계란찜, Gyeran Jjim) Recipe

Welcome back to Chelsea's K-Food Recipes! Today, we're making Korean Steamed Eggs (계란찜, Gyeran Jjim), a soft, moist side dish to scoop up alongside warm rice. 🥚

A little broth loosens the eggs, while green onion adds a fresh, savory finish. This version uses a small saucepan and a lid, so you can make it without a Korean earthenware pot.

The goal is tender, gently puffed eggs. Keep the heat low once they begin to set, and serve them right away for the best texture. Let's get cooking!

Ingredients

For 2 servings as a side dish:

  • 3 large eggs
  • 1/2 cup (120 ml) unsalted anchovy-kelp broth, vegetable broth, or water, cooled
  • 1/4 tsp fine salt
  • 1 green onion, finely sliced
  • 1 tsp neutral cooking oil, for the pan
  • 1/2 tsp sesame oil, optional
  • 1/2 tsp toasted sesame seeds, optional
  • Steamed rice, to serve

Equipment tip: Use a small, heavy-bottomed saucepan, about 14–16 cm wide, with a well-fitting lid. The uncooked mixture should fill it no more than halfway, leaving room for the eggs to rise.

Instructions

1. Mix the Eggs

Crack the eggs into a bowl and beat until the whites and yolks are evenly combined. Stir in the cooled broth or water and salt. For a more uniform texture, pass the mixture through a fine sieve before adding half the green onion.

2. Start over Medium-Low Heat

Brush the bottom and lower sides of the saucepan with the cooking oil. Pour in the egg mixture and place over medium-low heat.

Stir slowly with a silicone spatula, sweeping across the bottom and around the sides as small, soft curds form. This may take about 3–5 minutes; lower the heat if the bottom starts setting much faster than the rest.

3. Cover and Finish Gently

When the mixture is thickened but still loose, spread it evenly and sprinkle on the remaining green onion. Turn the heat to low and cover.

Cook for about 4–6 minutes, then lift the lid carefully, directing steam away from your face. The center should be set without liquid egg. Check the center with a food thermometer: egg dishes should reach 74°C (165°F), following Health Canada's egg safety guidance. If needed, cover and continue cooking over low heat, checking at one-minute intervals.

Timing varies with the pan and burner. Avoid raising the heat sharply to hurry the center along; the bottom can scorch before the middle finishes cooking.

4. Finish and Serve

Remove from the heat. Add the optional sesame oil and sesame seeds, and serve promptly with rice. A little settling after the lid comes off is normal.

Place the hot saucepan on a heatproof trivet, or spoon the eggs into serving bowls. Watch out for the hot pan and escaping steam!

Tips for Soft, Moist Eggs

  • Mix thoroughly: Break up streaks of egg white so the mixture sets more evenly.
  • Measure the liquid: Adding extra broth changes the texture and cooking time. Start with the amount listed here.
  • Keep additions small: Finely sliced green onion adds flavor without weighing down the eggs. Save chunky vegetables for another dish.
  • Stop when cooked: Continued heating can make the eggs firm and cause liquid to separate. Take the pan off the burner once the center reaches the safe temperature.

A Gentle Side for a Spicy Meal

Gyeran jjim is a savory Korean egg dish served as a side. Korean Bapsang's guide describes several cooking methods, including a water bath if you prefer cooking in a heatproof bowl.

Serve this saucepan version with rice and jeyuk bokkeum. The mild eggs make a pleasant accompaniment to the spicy pork. For another egg side dish, try gyeran mari, which rolls the eggs into layers you can slice.
