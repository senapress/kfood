Korean Inari Sushi (유부초밥, Yubu Chobap) Recipe

Welcome back to Chelsea's K-Food Recipes! Today, we're making one of the easiest and most nostalgic Korean lunchbox favorites, Korean Inari Sushi (유부초밥, Yubu Chobap)! 🍙🍱

This recipe is a little different from the dishes I've shared before because I'm going to tell you something very simple.

Just buy a yubu chobap kit from a Korean grocery store!

Seriously. That's how I've always made it.

Ever since I was a little kid, whenever my family made yubu chobap at home, we used one of those convenient kits from the Korean supermarket.

And even now, I still make it the same way.

Some foods are worth making completely from scratch, but honestly, yubu chobap is one of those dishes where the store-bought kit makes life so much easier.

If you've ever visited a Korean grocery store, you've probably seen those packages filled with sweet and savory tofu pockets, a small packet of vinegar seasoning, and a packet of dried vegetable flakes.

All you really need to add is freshly cooked rice.

That's it!

In Korea, yubu chobap is a popular choice for lunchboxes, picnics, school trips, and quick meals at home.

It's easy to pack, fun to eat, and doesn't require any complicated cooking techniques.

The sweet and slightly tangy tofu pockets pair perfectly with seasoned rice, making each bite deliciously satisfying.

And the best part?

You can make it in about 15 minutes if you already have cooked rice!

Let's make this childhood favorite the easy Korean way.

🍙 Ingredients

For Korean Inari Sushi (2-3 servings):

  • 1 package Korean yubu chobap kit (유부초밥 키트, usually makes about 12-16 pieces)
  • 2 bowls freshly cooked short-grain rice (about 400g cooked rice, or the amount specified on the package)

Usually Included in the Kit:

  • Seasoned fried tofu pockets (유부, yubu)
  • Sweet vinegar seasoning sauce
  • Dried vegetable and sesame seasoning mix

Optional Additions:

  • 1/2 small carrot, finely chopped
  • 1/4 cucumber, finely chopped
  • 1 scrambled egg, chopped
  • 2 tbsp canned tuna, drained
  • 1 tbsp mayonnaise (for tuna filling)
  • Extra sesame seeds
  • Chopped green onions

Note: Different brands include slightly different ingredients and make different numbers of pieces. Follow the rice quantity and seasoning instructions on your package for the best results.

👩‍🍳 Instructions

1️⃣ Buy a Yubu Chobap Kit

First, visit your local Korean grocery store.

Look for a package labeled 유부초밥 (Yubu Chobap) or Seasoned Fried Tofu Pockets for Sushi.

You can usually find these kits in the refrigerated section.

Most Korean yubu chobap kits contain everything you need except the rice.

Inside, you'll typically find seasoned tofu pockets, a vinegar seasoning packet, and a dried vegetable seasoning packet.

Honestly, this is the easiest and most familiar way to make yubu chobap in many Korean households.

It's how I've been eating it since childhood!

2️⃣ Prepare the Rice

Cook short-grain white rice according to your usual method.

Korean short-grain rice works especially well because it has a slightly sticky texture that helps the filling stay together.

Transfer about 400g of freshly cooked rice to a large mixing bowl, or use the amount recommended on your kit.

Let the rice cool slightly so it's warm rather than steaming hot.

You don't want the rice to be completely cold, but it shouldn't be so hot that it's difficult to handle.

3️⃣ Season the Rice

Open the vinegar seasoning packet included in your kit.

Pour it over the warm rice.

Add the dried vegetable and sesame seasoning mix.

Using a rice paddle or spatula, gently fold everything together.

Try not to mash the rice.

The goal is to distribute the seasoning evenly while keeping the rice grains intact.

Taste the rice and adjust according to your preference, keeping in mind that the tofu pockets are already seasoned.

The rice should taste lightly sweet, tangy, and savory.

4️⃣ Prepare the Tofu Pockets

Carefully open the package of seasoned fried tofu pockets.

The pockets are usually packed in a sweet and savory liquid.

Gently squeeze each pocket to remove a little excess liquid.

Don't squeeze too hard!

You want the tofu pockets to stay moist and flavorful.

Carefully open each pocket with your fingers to create space for the rice filling.

Be gentle because the tofu can tear if you pull too hard.

5️⃣ Fill the Tofu Pockets

Take a small amount of seasoned rice and gently shape it with your hands or a spoon.

Carefully place the rice inside a tofu pocket.

Press lightly so the rice fills the pocket without tearing it.

Repeat until all the pockets are filled.

You don't need to pack the rice too tightly.

A slightly fluffy filling makes the yubu chobap more enjoyable to eat.

This is also a fun step to do with kids!

I remember yubu chobap being one of those foods that always felt familiar and comforting growing up.

There's something satisfying about filling each little tofu pocket with rice.

6️⃣ Add Optional Fillings

If you want to make your yubu chobap a little more special, try mixing additional ingredients into the rice.

Finely chopped carrots, cucumbers, or scrambled eggs add color and texture.

For a tuna version, mix drained canned tuna with a little mayonnaise and add it to the rice or spoon it on top.

You can also sprinkle extra sesame seeds over the finished pieces.

But honestly?

The basic version made straight from the kit is already delicious.

That's the version I grew up eating, and it's still my favorite way to make it.

7️⃣ Serve and Enjoy

Arrange the finished yubu chobap neatly on a plate or in a lunchbox.

Serve immediately, or pack it for a picnic or an easy lunch.

Yubu chobap is delicious at room temperature, which makes it especially convenient for lunchboxes.

Pair it with kimchi, fresh fruit, or a simple Korean soup.

And that's it!

Your homemade Korean yubu chobap is ready to enjoy.

💡 Tips for Perfect Korean Yubu Chobap

  • ✅ Buy a Korean grocery store kit: This is the easiest and most convenient way to make authentic Korean home-style yubu chobap.
  • ✅ Follow the package instructions: Each brand may have slightly different seasoning amounts and recommended rice quantities.
  • ✅ Use Korean short-grain rice: Its slightly sticky texture helps the rice hold together inside the tofu pockets.
  • ✅ Don't overmix the rice: Gently fold the seasonings into the rice to keep the texture light and fluffy.
  • ✅ Don't squeeze the tofu pockets too hard: Removing a little excess liquid is enough. Keeping some moisture makes them flavorful.
  • ✅ Avoid overstuffing: Fill the pockets gently so they don't tear.
  • ✅ Try fun variations: Tuna mayo, scrambled eggs, carrots, or cucumber are easy additions.
  • ✅ Pack safely for lunch: If you're not eating it right away, refrigerate it promptly and use an insulated lunch bag with an ice pack when taking it out.
  • ✅ Keep it simple: You really don't need to add anything fancy. The classic kit version is delicious on its own!
