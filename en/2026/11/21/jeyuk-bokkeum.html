Korean Spicy Stir-Fried Pork (제육볶음, Jeyuk Bokkeum) Recipe

Welcome back to Chelsea's K-Food Recipes! Today, we're making Korean Spicy Stir-Fried Pork (제육볶음, Jeyuk Bokkeum), with thin slices of pork, tender onion, and a glossy red sauce. 🌶️

This version brings together gochujang, garlic, and a little sweetness. Serve it with warm rice, or tuck a few pieces into lettuce with a spoonful of rice for a delicious bite.

The key is to give the pork enough room in the pan and control the heat so the sauce coats the meat without scorching. Let's get cooking!

Ingredients

For 2–3 servings with rice:

  • 400 g boneless pork shoulder, sliced across the grain about 3 mm thick and cut into bite-sized pieces
  • 1/2 medium onion, thinly sliced
  • 2 green onions, cut into 4 cm lengths
  • 2 tsp neutral cooking oil, divided
  • 1 tsp sesame oil
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds, optional
  • Steamed rice and washed lettuce leaves, to serve

For the sauce:

  • 2 tbsp gochujang (Korean red chili paste)
  • 1 tbsp gochugaru (Korean red pepper flakes), or 1 tsp for less heat
  • 1 tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • 1 tbsp minced garlic
  • 1 tsp grated fresh ginger
  • 2 tbsp water
  • A pinch of black pepper

Ingredient tip: Thinly sliced pork shoulder has a useful balance of lean meat and fat. Gochujang varies in heat and saltiness, so use a paste you know if possible. Reducing the gochugaru makes the dish less hot, but the gochujang still adds heat.

Instructions

1. Prepare the Pork and Vegetables

Separate any pork slices that are stuck together. Slice the onion and green onions before heating the pan, and keep the lettuce and other ready-to-eat ingredients away from raw pork and its utensils.

2. Mix the Sauce and Marinate

Stir all the sauce ingredients together in a bowl. Add the pork and mix until evenly coated. Cover and refrigerate for 20–30 minutes while you prepare the rice and serving dishes. Don't taste the sauce after it has touched raw pork.

3. Cook in Two Batches

Heat a wide frying pan over medium-high heat, then add 1 teaspoon oil. Add half the pork with its coating of sauce and half the sliced onion. Spread everything out, separating the pork slices with tongs.

Cook for about 4–6 minutes, turning and stirring frequently, until the pork is cooked through and the onion softens. Lower the heat to medium if the sauce starts to darken too quickly. If it catches on the pan, add water a tablespoon at a time and loosen it with a spatula.

Check the thickest pieces with a thin-tip food thermometer inserted sideways. Cook pork to 71°C (160°F), following Health Canada's safe cooking temperatures. Timing is only a guide; thickness and pan heat will affect it.

Transfer the cooked batch to a clean plate. Repeat with the remaining teaspoon of oil, pork, and onion, cooking this batch to the same temperature.

4. Add the Green Onions

Return the first batch to the pan and add the green onions. Toss for 1–2 minutes, until the green onions soften slightly and everything is hot. The sauce should cling to the pork rather than form a deep pool.

Turn off the heat and stir in the sesame oil. Sprinkle with the optional sesame seeds.

5. Serve with Rice or Lettuce

Serve hot with rice and lettuce on the side. For a lettuce wrap, add a little rice and pork to a leaf, fold it around the filling, and enjoy. Keep the filling small enough to eat comfortably!

Tips for a Flavorful Stir-Fry

  • Keep the slices thin: Thick chunks need longer cooking and can leave the sauce scorched before the meat is ready.
  • Use batches: A crowded pan releases more liquid and makes it harder to get a coating that clings to the pork.
  • Watch the sauce: Gochujang and sugar can burn. Adjust the heat as you cook instead of leaving the burner on its highest setting.
  • Adjust next time: If this batch is too spicy, serve smaller portions with more rice. For the next batch, reduce the gochugaru first.

A Spicy Main Dish for a Korean Meal

Jeyuk bokkeum pairs pork with a spicy seasoning; Korean Bapsang's introduction describes its relationship to spicy pork bulgogi and offers another preparation.

For this meal, add Korean rolled omelette and a small bowl of soybean paste stew. The soft eggs and warm broth give you different textures alongside the pork.
