Korean Sweet Pancakes (호떡, Hotteok) Recipe

Welcome back to Chelsea's K-Food Recipes! Today, we're making Korean Sweet Pancakes (호떡, Hotteok), with golden edges and a warm sugar-and-nut filling. 🥞

Hotteok is a Korean street snack made by wrapping sweet filling in dough and flattening it in a frying pan. Korean Bapsang's guide describes this yeast-dough style and explains how sweet rice flour can add chewiness. This version keeps the dough simple with all-purpose flour.

Set aside time for the dough to rise, then cook a few pancakes at a time. The filling gets very hot, so let each one cool a little before taking a bite!

Ingredients

Makes 8 pancakes:

For the dough:

  • 2 cups (250 g) all-purpose flour
  • 2 tbsp granulated sugar
  • 1 tsp instant yeast
  • 1/2 tsp fine salt
  • 3/4 cup (180 ml) lukewarm water, plus 1–2 tbsp if needed
  • 1 tbsp neutral cooking oil

For the filling:

  • 1/3 cup packed light brown sugar
  • 3 tbsp finely chopped walnuts
  • 1/2 tsp ground cinnamon

For shaping and frying:

  • About 3 tbsp neutral cooking oil, used as needed

Equipment: A nonstick frying pan with a lid, a wide heatproof spatula, and a mixing bowl.

Instructions

1. Make a Soft Dough

Mix the flour, granulated sugar, instant yeast, and salt in the bowl. Add the lukewarm water and 1 tbsp oil. Stir, then knead in the bowl for about 3–5 minutes, until the dough is cohesive and stretchy.

It should feel soft and tacky. If dry flour remains after mixing, add extra water a teaspoon at a time, up to the additional amount listed. Use comfortably warm water and follow the temperature guidance on your yeast packet.

2. Let It Rise

Cover the bowl and leave it in a warm spot until the dough is roughly doubled, about 60–90 minutes. A cool kitchen may take longer; judge by the dough's size rather than the clock.

Gently press out the large air bubbles, fold the dough over itself, and let it rest, covered, for another 15 minutes.

3. Fill and Seal

Mix the brown sugar, walnuts, and cinnamon. Divide the filling into eight portions so each pancake gets an even share.

Lightly oil your hands and a tray, then divide the dough into eight pieces. Flatten one piece into a palm-sized disk, leaving the center slightly thicker. Put one portion of filling in the middle. Gather the edges over it and pinch firmly closed, keeping sugar away from the seam. Place on the tray seam-side down and repeat.

4. Fry in Small Batches

Heat a thin coating of oil in the pan over medium-low heat. Add two or three filled dough balls seam-side down, leaving room to flatten them. Cook for about 1 minute, until the bottoms begin to turn golden.

Flip with the spatula, then press gently into disks about 9–10 cm wide. Avoid pressing so hard that the filling breaks through. Cook for another 1–2 minutes, flip again, and lower the heat. Cover for 1–2 minutes to help the dough cook through and the sugar melt.

The pancakes should be golden on both sides, with no sticky, raw dough inside. Check the first pancake by cutting it open carefully on a plate; extend the covered cooking time over low heat if needed. Add a little oil between batches. If the outside browns too quickly, lower the heat before continuing.

5. Cool Briefly and Serve

Transfer to a plate and let the pancakes cool for a few minutes. Cut one open carefully to check the filling before serving, especially to children. The melted sugar can stay hot even when the surface feels comfortable to touch.

Tips for Easier Shaping

  • Oil your hands lightly: This helps with sticky dough without working in lots of extra flour.
  • Chop the nuts finely: Large pieces can puncture the dough when you flatten it.
  • Keep the seam clean: Sugar caught between the edges makes sealing harder.
  • Use the first pancake as a guide: Adjust the burner and covered cooking time before frying the rest.
