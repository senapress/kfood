Korean Soybean Paste Stew (된장찌개, Doenjang Jjigae) Recipe

Welcome back to Chelsea's K-Food Recipes! Today, we're making Korean Soybean Paste Stew (된장찌개, Doenjang Jjigae), a warm, savory dish to enjoy with a bowl of rice. 🍲

Doenjang, Korean fermented soybean paste, gives the broth its earthy flavor. Soft tofu, tender potato, and zucchini turn a small pot of stew into a satisfying part of a home-cooked meal.

There are many ways to make it. This version keeps things simple with vegetables and tofu, plus your choice of unsalted anchovy-kelp broth or vegetable broth.

Let's get cooking!

Ingredients

For 2 servings with rice:

  • 2 cups (480 ml) unsalted anchovy-kelp broth or vegetable broth
  • 2 tbsp doenjang (된장), plus a little more if needed
  • 1 small potato (about 100 g), peeled and cut into 1 cm cubes
  • 1/2 small onion, cut into bite-sized pieces
  • 1/2 small zucchini (about 100 g), cut into half-moons about 1 cm thick
  • 2 fresh shiitake mushrooms, tough stems removed, caps sliced
  • 200 g medium-firm or firm tofu, drained and cut into 2 cm cubes
  • 1 tsp minced garlic
  • 1 green onion, sliced
  • 1/2 tsp gochugaru (Korean red pepper flakes), optional
  • 1 fresh green chili, sliced, optional
  • Steamed rice, to serve

Ingredient tip: Choose plain doenjang rather than ssamjang, a seasoned dipping paste. For a vegetarian version, use vegetable broth and check that the paste contains no seafood ingredients.

Instructions

1. Prepare the Vegetables and Tofu

Cut the potato small so it cooks before the softer vegetables become mushy. Prepare the onion, zucchini, mushrooms, tofu, and green onion before turning on the stove.

A small saucepan works well. You don't need an earthenware pot to make this stew!

2. Dissolve the Doenjang

Bring the broth to a boil in the pot. Stir in 2 tablespoons doenjang, pressing any large lumps against the side with a spoon.

Taste a little broth after letting it cool on the spoon. Doenjang varies in saltiness, so wait until the end before adding more.

3. Cook the Potato and Onion

Add the potato and onion. Lower the heat to a steady simmer and cook, partially covered, for 7–9 minutes, until the potato is almost tender.

The timing depends on the size of the pieces. Check one with a fork rather than relying only on the clock.

4. Add the Zucchini and Mushrooms

Add the zucchini, mushrooms, garlic, and optional gochugaru. Simmer for 4–5 minutes, until the zucchini softens and the potato is tender all the way through.

Keep the stew gently bubbling. If too much liquid evaporates, add a small splash of water.

5. Finish with Tofu and Green Onion

Gently slide in the tofu, then add the green onion and optional chili. Simmer for 3–4 minutes, until the tofu is hot throughout. Stir carefully to keep the cubes intact.

Taste again. If the broth needs more flavor, dissolve an extra teaspoon of doenjang in a little hot broth and stir it back in. If it tastes too salty, add water a little at a time.

6. Serve with Rice

Serve hot alongside steamed rice and a few side dishes. Let each spoonful cool enough to eat, especially if the stew is served in a pot that retains heat.

Tips for a Flavorful Pot

  • Keep the broth modest: This is a stew to eat with rice, so the vegetables and tofu shouldn't disappear in a large volume of liquid.
  • Add ingredients in stages: Potato needs a head start; tofu only needs gentle heating at the end.
  • Adjust the heat: Leave out both chili ingredients for a version without added chili heat.
  • Season at the end: Broth becomes more concentrated as it simmers. Taste before adding extra paste or salt.

A Familiar Korean Home-Cooked Dish

Doenjang jjigae is a staple of Korean home cooking. Tofu and vegetables are common additions, while other versions include beef or seafood. Korea.net's introduction and recipe offers another example of this familiar dish.
