Korean Fish Cake Soup (어묵탕, Eomuk Tang) Recipe

Welcome back to Chelsea's K-Food Recipes! Today, we're making Korean Fish Cake Soup (어묵탕, Eomuk Tang), a warming pot of tender fish cakes and radish in a savory broth. 🍲

Fish cakes simmering at a Korean street stall are a familiar winter sight. VISITKOREA's introduction to eomuk describes the winter snack and its anchovy-and-radish broth. At home, a saucepan and a few bowls are all you need to enjoy those flavors.

This version uses bite-sized fish cakes, with radish cooked until tender before the fish cakes go in. Keep the seasoning light at first, then taste the finished soup.

Ingredients

For 2 generous servings:

  • 200 g Korean fish cakes (eomuk, 어묵), thawed according to the package if frozen
  • 200 g Korean radish (mu, 무), peeled
  • 4 cups (960 ml) unsalted anchovy-kelp broth
  • 1 tsp minced garlic
  • 1 tsp Korean soup soy sauce (guk-ganjang), plus more if needed
  • 1 green onion, sliced diagonally
  • 1 fresh green chili, sliced, optional
  • A small pinch of ground black pepper
  • Salt, only if needed
  • Steamed rice, to serve, optional

Ingredient tip: Choose Korean fish cakes intended for soup or stir-frying. Flat sheets and thicker pieces both work, but may need different cooking times. Check the package for preparation instructions and allergens; ingredients vary by brand. If using seasoned broth, reduce or omit the initial soy sauce.

Instructions

1. Prepare the Radish and Fish Cakes

Cut the radish into squares about 2–3 cm wide and 5 mm thick. Cut the fish cakes into pieces that fit comfortably on a spoon.

If the fish cakes have a very oily surface, briefly blanch them in a separate pot of boiling water, then drain. This is optional; follow any preparation directions on the package.

2. Simmer the Radish

Bring the broth to a boil in a medium saucepan. Add the radish, lower the heat, and simmer gently for 8–12 minutes, until the edges look translucent and a fork slides through easily.

Give the radish more time if it is still firm. Cooking it first lets you soften it without keeping the fish cakes in the pot too long.

3. Add the Fish Cakes

Add the fish cakes, garlic, and 1 teaspoon soup soy sauce. Return to a gentle simmer and cook for about 4–6 minutes, or as directed on the fish cake package, until the pieces are hot throughout and tender.

Thicker fish cakes may take longer than thin sheets. Keep the bubbling gentle and check their texture; prolonged boiling can make them overly soft.

4. Finish and Adjust the Broth

Add the green onion, optional chili, and black pepper. Simmer for another 1 minute.

Let a spoonful cool, then taste. Add a little more soup soy sauce or a pinch of salt only if needed, since the fish cakes contribute seasoning. If the broth tastes too salty or has reduced too much, add a splash of water and heat through.

5. Serve in Bowls

Ladle the radish, fish cakes, and broth into bowls. Serve warm, with steamed rice if you want a simple meal. Let each spoonful cool enough to eat.

Tips for a Clear, Savory Soup

  • Start with unsalted broth: It gives you more control over the final seasoning.
  • Check the radish before adding fish cakes: Tender radish makes the soup easier to eat, while fish cakes need only a short simmer.
  • Make chili optional: Leave it out for a mild bowl, or add a few slices for a little heat.
  • Cook what you plan to serve: Fish cakes continue absorbing broth as they sit, so their texture changes when left in the soup.

Bring a Little Street-Stall Warmth Home

This bowl is also often called eomuk guk (어묵국). For another home-style approach, Korean Bapsang's fish cake soup recipe uses radish and anchovy broth, too.

Enjoy it alongside spicy rice cakes, tteokbokki, or pair it with rolled omelette, gyeran mari and rice for a gentle, savory meal.
