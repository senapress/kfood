Korean Pan-Fried Bread Pizza (식빵 피자, Sikppang Pizza) Recipe

Welcome back to Chelsea's K-Food Recipes! Today, we're making something extra special and nostalgic, Korean Pan-Fried Bread Pizza (식빵 피자, Sikppang Pizza)! 🍕🍞

This simple homemade pizza brings back warm childhood memories of my grandmother making delicious snacks for me in her kitchen.

Instead of using traditional pizza dough or an oven, she would take a slice of ordinary sandwich bread, spread some ketchup on top, add colorful vegetables, ham, and cheese, and cook everything in a frying pan.

The bottom of the bread would turn golden and crispy while the cheese slowly melted on top. The smell alone was enough to make me run into the kitchen!

It wasn't fancy, but somehow, it tasted better than any pizza from a restaurant.

Today, I'm sharing this easy, old-fashioned Korean-style bread pizza that reminds me of those happy afternoons at Grandma's house.

Let's get cooking!

🍕 Ingredients

For the Bread Pizza:

  • 4 slices sandwich bread (식빵, sikppang)
  • 4 tbsp ketchup
  • 1/2 onion, finely chopped
  • 1/2 green bell pepper, finely chopped
  • 1/4 carrot, finely chopped
  • 2 slices ham, cut into small pieces
  • 1/4 cup canned sweet corn, drained
  • 1 cup shredded mozzarella cheese
  • 1 tbsp butter or vegetable oil

Optional Toppings:

  • Sliced sausages
  • Mushrooms, thinly sliced
  • Red or yellow bell peppers
  • A little mayonnaise
  • Dried parsley or oregano
  • A pinch of black pepper

👩‍🍳 Instructions

1️⃣ Prepare the Vegetables

Finely chop the onion, bell pepper, and carrot into small pieces.

Cut the ham into bite-sized squares.

Drain the canned sweet corn thoroughly to remove excess moisture.

If using mushrooms or sausages, slice them into small pieces as well.

Keeping the toppings small helps them cook evenly and makes the pizza easier to eat.

2️⃣ Lightly Cook the Toppings

Heat a small frying pan over medium heat with a little butter or vegetable oil.

Add the chopped onion, carrot, and bell pepper.

Stir-fry for 2-3 minutes until slightly softened.

Add the ham and sweet corn, then cook for another minute.

Remove from the heat and set aside.

This step helps remove excess moisture and ensures the vegetables are tender before assembling the pizza.

3️⃣ Prepare the Bread

Place the sandwich bread slices on a clean cutting board.

Spread 1 tablespoon of ketchup evenly over each slice.

Make sure to cover most of the surface, leaving a small border around the edges.

If you like a slightly sweeter, creamier flavor, add a small drizzle of mayonnaise.

This simple ketchup-based sauce is what gives old-fashioned Korean bread pizza its familiar childhood taste!

4️⃣ Add the Toppings

Divide the cooked vegetables, ham, and sweet corn evenly among the bread slices.

Spread the toppings across the bread.

Sprinkle a generous amount of shredded mozzarella cheese on top.

Make sure the cheese covers the vegetables so everything stays together as it melts.

For an extra cheesy version, add a little more mozzarella!

5️⃣ Cook the Pizza in a Frying Pan

Heat a large nonstick frying pan over low heat.

Add a small amount of butter or vegetable oil.

Carefully place the prepared bread pizzas into the pan.

Cover with a lid and cook for approximately 5-7 minutes.

The low heat allows the cheese to melt slowly without burning the bottom of the bread.

Check occasionally to make sure the bread is turning golden brown rather than burning.

Once the cheese is fully melted and the bottom is crispy, remove the pizzas from the pan.

If the cheese needs more time, add a few drops of water to an empty area of the pan, away from the bread, and immediately cover with the lid for a short time.

The steam will help melt the cheese.

6️⃣ Slice and Serve

Transfer the bread pizzas to a cutting board.

Let them cool for about 1 minute before slicing.

Cut each slice diagonally into two triangles or into four smaller squares.

Sprinkle with dried parsley or oregano if desired.

Serve immediately while the bread is crispy and the cheese is warm and stretchy.

Enjoy your homemade Korean-style bread pizza!

💡 Tips for Perfect Korean Bread Pizza

  • ✅ Use ordinary sandwich bread: Soft white sandwich bread gives this recipe the classic homemade taste I remember from childhood.
  • ✅ Cook over low heat: High heat can burn the bread before the cheese melts. A covered frying pan and gentle heat work best.
  • ✅ Don't overload the toppings: Too many vegetables can make the bread soggy. A thin, even layer works perfectly.
  • ✅ Use ketchup for the nostalgic flavor: Traditional pizza sauce is delicious, but simple ketchup gives this snack its familiar Korean home-style taste.
  • ✅ Add mayonnaise for extra flavor: A small drizzle of mayonnaise adds a creamy, slightly sweet taste.
  • ✅ Try different toppings: Sausages, mushrooms, leftover vegetables, or even small pieces of bacon work well.
  • ✅ Enjoy immediately: Bread pizza tastes best straight from the pan while the bottom is crispy and the cheese is melted.

Korean Pan-Fried Bread Pizza (식빵 피자) is more than just an easy snack. For me, it's a little piece of childhood.

I still remember watching my grandmother prepare everything in her kitchen. There was no pizza oven, no homemade dough, and no expensive ingredients. Just a few slices of bread, whatever vegetables were in the refrigerator, some ketchup, and cheese.

She would carefully place the bread in a frying pan, cover it with a lid, and wait for the cheese to melt.

Those few minutes always felt so long when I was little!

When the pizza was finally ready, the bread was perfectly crispy on the bottom, the cheese was soft and melted, and the ketchup added just the right amount of sweetness.

It was such a simple snack, but it was made with so much love.

Even now, whenever I make this bread pizza, the familiar smell reminds me of my grandmother and those peaceful afternoons at home.

Sometimes, the most memorable foods aren't the complicated ones. They're the simple dishes made by someone who loves us.

I hope this recipe brings a little warmth and nostalgia to your kitchen, too.
