Korean Stir-Fried Eggplant (가지볶음, Gaji Bokkeum) Recipe

Welcome back to Chelsea's K-Food Recipes! Today, we're making a simple but incredibly delicious Korean side dish, Korean Stir-Fried Eggplant (가지볶음, Gaji Bokkeum)! 🍆🍚

If you've ever eaten a traditional Korean home-cooked meal, you probably know that the side dishes, called Banchan (반찬), are just as important as the main dish.

A Korean dinner table often includes a bowl of rice, a warm soup, and several small plates of vegetables, kimchi, or other delicious side dishes.

And one of my favorite ways to turn an ordinary vegetable into something special is this easy stir-fried eggplant.

Now, I know eggplant can be a little controversial.

Some people love its soft, silky texture, while others aren't quite sure what to do with it.

But Korean-style eggplant is definitely worth trying!

When stir-fried with garlic, soy sauce, and sesame oil, eggplant becomes tender, savory, and full of flavor.

It absorbs the delicious seasoning beautifully, making every bite rich and satisfying.

The best part?

You only need a few ingredients and about 15 minutes!

It's an affordable, easy, and comforting Korean side dish that goes perfectly with a bowl of freshly cooked rice.

Let's get cooking!

🍆 Ingredients

For the Stir-Fried Eggplant (2-3 servings):

  • 2 medium eggplants (가지, gaji)
  • 1/2 onion, thinly sliced
  • 1 green onion, chopped
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 tsp oyster sauce (optional)
  • 1 tsp sesame oil
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds
  • 2-3 tbsp water
  • Black pepper, to taste

Optional Additions:

  • 1/2 tsp Korean red pepper flakes (고춧가루, gochugaru)
  • 1 small red chili pepper, sliced
  • 1 tsp sugar or honey
  • 1/2 tsp Korean chili paste (고추장, gochujang)
  • Extra chopped green onions

👩‍🍳 Instructions

1️⃣ Prepare the Eggplant

Wash the eggplants thoroughly under running water.

Trim off the stems and cut each eggplant into bite-sized pieces.

You can slice them into thick half-moons or cut them into long strips.

Try to keep the pieces similar in size so they cook evenly.

If you're using large eggplants, cut them into slightly smaller pieces to help them cook faster.

There's no need to peel the eggplant.

The purple skin adds beautiful color and helps the pieces hold their shape during cooking.

2️⃣ Prepare the Vegetables

Thinly slice the onion.

Chop the green onion into small pieces.

Mince the garlic cloves.

If you like a little heat, slice a red chili pepper or prepare some Korean red pepper flakes.

Set everything aside.

3️⃣ Make the Seasoning Sauce

In a small bowl, combine:

  • 1 tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 tsp oyster sauce (optional)
  • 2 tbsp water
  • A pinch of black pepper

Mix well.

If you prefer a slightly sweeter flavor, add 1 teaspoon of sugar or honey.

For a spicy version, add Korean red pepper flakes.

Preparing the sauce in advance helps the eggplant cook evenly without burning the seasonings.

4️⃣ Stir-Fry the Vegetables

Heat 1 tablespoon of vegetable oil in a large frying pan over medium heat.

Add the sliced onion and stir-fry for 1-2 minutes, until slightly softened.

Add the minced garlic and chopped green onion.

Stir-fry for another 30 seconds, until fragrant.

Be careful not to burn the garlic.

5️⃣ Cook the Eggplant

Add the prepared eggplant pieces to the frying pan.

Stir-fry over medium heat for 3-4 minutes, gently tossing to coat them with the oil and aromatics.

Eggplant absorbs oil quickly, so don't worry if the pan looks a little dry.

Instead of adding too much oil, pour in the prepared seasoning sauce.

Stir gently to coat the eggplant evenly.

Cover the pan with a lid and cook for another 3-5 minutes.

The steam helps soften the eggplant without requiring a lot of extra oil.

If the pan becomes too dry, add another tablespoon of water.

Continue cooking until the eggplant is tender and silky but still holds its shape.

6️⃣ Finish with Sesame Oil

Remove the lid and gently stir the eggplant.

Allow any excess liquid to evaporate for about 1 minute.

Turn off the heat.

Drizzle 1 teaspoon of sesame oil over the eggplant.

Sprinkle with toasted sesame seeds and a little extra chopped green onion.

Gently toss everything together.

Your Korean stir-fried eggplant is ready!

7️⃣ Serve as Korean Banchan

Transfer the stir-fried eggplant to a small serving plate.

Sprinkle with a few extra sesame seeds if desired.

Serve warm or at room temperature alongside freshly cooked white rice.

This dish pairs wonderfully with kimchi, grilled fish, bulgogi, or a simple bowl of Korean soup.

Enjoy your homemade Gaji Bokkeum!

💡 Tips for Perfect Korean Stir-Fried Eggplant

  • ✅ Choose fresh eggplants: Look for eggplants with smooth, shiny skin and a firm texture. Smaller, tender eggplants often have fewer seeds.
  • ✅ Don't use too much oil: Eggplant absorbs oil very quickly. Adding a little water and covering the pan helps soften it without making the dish greasy.
  • ✅ Avoid overcooking: The eggplant should be tender and silky but not completely mushy.
  • ✅ Use sesame oil at the end: Adding sesame oil after turning off the heat preserves its rich, nutty aroma.
  • ✅ Adjust the seasoning: Add more soy sauce for a saltier flavor or a little sugar for a sweeter Korean home-style taste.
  • ✅ Make it spicy: Add gochugaru or sliced chili peppers for a delicious spicy variation.
  • ✅ Keep it vegetarian: Skip the oyster sauce or replace it with a vegetarian mushroom-based sauce.
  • ✅ Store leftovers properly: Refrigerate in an airtight container for up to 3 days. Enjoy cold, at room temperature, or gently reheated.

Korean Stir-Fried Eggplant (가지볶음, Gaji Bokkeum) is one of those humble side dishes that perfectly represents everyday Korean home cooking.

It doesn't require expensive ingredients or complicated techniques.

Just a couple of eggplants, some garlic, soy sauce, and sesame oil.

But somehow, these simple ingredients create something incredibly comforting.

In Korea, we have a wonderful food culture built around Banchan (반찬), the small side dishes that accompany rice and soup.

Sometimes, a Korean meal doesn't even need a fancy main dish.

A bowl of warm rice, a simple soup, some kimchi, and two or three homemade vegetable side dishes can be more than enough.

And this stir-fried eggplant fits perfectly into that kind of meal.

I especially love how the eggplant absorbs the savory soy sauce and garlic while becoming soft and silky.

It's delicious with rice, and the sesame oil adds that familiar nutty aroma found in so many Korean home-cooked dishes.

If you've never been a big fan of eggplant, this recipe might just change your mind.

Sometimes, all it takes is a different cooking method to discover how delicious a simple vegetable can be.

So the next time you have a couple of eggplants sitting in your refrigerator, try making this easy Korean side dish.

A little soy sauce, garlic, sesame oil, and 15 minutes. That's all you need for a delicious plate of Korean banchan!
